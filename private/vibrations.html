1 · How to run the business from California

The LLC is organized in Hawaii and taxed as an S corp. The store is closing in Hawaii and the business continues in California, and the owners are moving too.

Confirm the city first. Notes from last week said Palm Springs (Riverside County); today you said Malibu (Los Angeles County). The county decides where the DBA is filed, the sales-tax rate and the city license.

Three ways to do it

A · Convert the Hawaii LLC into a California LLC (my lean)

One entity just changes its home state. Keeps the EIN, the S election, bank history and the books. California files the conversion as Articles of Organization – Conversion (LLC-1A); Hawaii's side ends the Hawaii registration. Federally this is a non-event. Only works if Hawaii law allows the outbound conversion, so confirm with DCCA first.

B · Keep the Hawaii LLC and register it in California as foreign

Fastest: one California filing (LLC-5). But they keep paying and filing in Hawaii every year (annual report, registered agent) for a company that no longer does business there. Fine as a stopgap if the CA store must open before the conversion clears.

C · Form a new California LLC and move the business into it

Clean slate, but new EIN, new Form 2553, new bank and merchant accounts, and moving the assets has to be planned so it isn't a taxable sale (depreciation recapture on fixtures). Then dissolve the Hawaii LLC. Most paperwork.

State information: Hawaii and California

Does Hawaii's GE need to be unwound, and what does California have instead?

Short answer: California has no GE tax. Its closest match is sales and use tax, run by CDTFA through a free seller's permit, collected from the customer at the register (a retail store is the textbook case). On top of that, the company pays California franchise tax as an S corp. So yes: close the Hawaii GE license once the last Hawaii sale is reported, and open a CDTFA seller's permit before the first California sale.
