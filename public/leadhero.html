Where I'm coming from

This is my third AEP. The first two were spent in group enrollment, focused on unions. Working under another agent's LLC, we closed four unions with more than 15,000 members, and my side of the arrangement was the non-union family members around them.

It worked, but it was short-term and high-risk. The work happened at the level of the union and the company rather than the person, and the culture and some of the methods weren't ones I wanted my name on. I was also building most of it from scratch: the website, local compliance, the meetings, and training recruits who didn't really want to do Medicare.

Walking away took some of the wind out of me. What brought it back is the opposite kind of work: one person, one kitchen table, one plan that actually fits. That's the book I'm building now, and I want to build it inside a real agency, with real systems, and unlearn the habits group work taught me.

How I'm positioned

Three hats, one person. Each one opens a door the others can't.

The advocate

helpinghawaiiseniors.com · eric@helpinghawaiiseniors.com

A plain-language news and education site for Hawaii seniors, with a blog that explains what's changing and who it hits, starting with the HMSA Akamai closures (about 16,000 members).

It's also my lead collector. I advertise the site in a magazine and online, and every visitor who asks for help comes in through its Contact me form, booking calendar or RetireFlo intake, already tagged in GHL. The alias email keeps the conversation in the advocate's voice, so people meet the helper before they meet the salesperson.

The agent

Resident license, Hawaii · Producer 491278 · NPN 19168935 · Non-resident: Washington (1187692), Nevada (health), Virginia

Appointed through IFG with Devoted, UHC and Humana. This is where the advocate conversation turns into a Scope of Appointment, a plan review and an enrollment. RetireFlo intake feeds GHL, every prospect is tagged with where we met, and a daily call list tells me who's owed a call.

The CPA

Full-scope practice

Tax preparation and planning, bookkeeping, advisory, and the money questions that sit around Medicare: IRMAA surcharges, Social Security timing, RMDs, retirement income, and helping families through a parent's paperwork. A CPA is already a trusted name at the table, so Medicare becomes part of a relationship rather than a cold pitch.

Prospecting hour

An hour of phone calls, every weekday

A call list is built each morning from GHL: kiosk leads inside 48 hours, promised call-backs, people waiting on a plan comparison, and new T65 and event leads. Every call is recorded, with identity check, the TPMO disclaimer and a Scope of Appointment before any plan talk, and the outcome is logged back to GHL.

How I invest

I'm willing to spend out of pocket, and I keep operations thin.

  • Carriers first. Before I pay for a venue, I ask a carrier to sponsor it. The Devoted kiosks are the model: free table, real foot traffic.
  • No retail space until we have to scale. A storefront is overhead before it's a lead source. Kitchen tables, libraries, partner offices and carrier kiosks come first.
  • Built, not bought. I designed the website and blog myself, set up the GHL pipelines and tagging, and run my own follow-up system, so most of what I spend goes to reaching people, not software.
  • Self-funded so far: event tables and sponsorships, community mail, the ReminderMedia program, the website and its hosting, and my licensing and certifications.

The co-op money

Thank you for sending marketing dollars. I plan to put it where I can measure it: event tables, carrier-approved mail with a tracking code, and kiosk materials. I'll report leads, appointments and enrollments back against every dollar.

Where IFG's reach is thin here

IFG doesn't hold the Walmart contract in Hawaii (another agency has it), so the big-box retail play isn't open to us. That's why the carrier-sponsored Longs kiosks, community organizations and the CPA practice carry so much weight in this plan.

    Where I need help

    Follow-up

    I generate conversations faster than I close the loop on them. I want a cadence I don't have to reinvent: who gets touched, when, and how, and the discipline to run it during AEP.

    Closing warm leads

    When I get someone to an in-person meeting, I close at a high rate. The gap is getting warm leads from "call me later" to a kitchen-table appointment.

    Training

    I want IFG's training and systems: a sales process I can repeat from first call to enrollment, carrier and product updates each season, and compliance I don't have to piece together myself. A ride-along on a kiosk day would help most.
